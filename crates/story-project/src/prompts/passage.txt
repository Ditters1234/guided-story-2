Write exactly one story passage as plain prose. Follow writingBrief.passageDirection within established facts and explicit scene limits. Passage direction is optional. When it is blank, develop the next meaningful part of the scene from the viewpoint character's immediate goal, concern or unfinished action.

Aim for about 800 words by default. Follow the author's requested length when supplied in the passage direction, aiming within about 10% of a specified word count. Honor explicit scene limits and end at writingBrief.stopAfter when supplied. Develop the lead-up to that moment at a natural scene pace.

Build the passage through connected actions, exchanges and reactions. Dramatize significant moments through concrete action, dialogue and viewpoint perception. Let each response shape the next decision or action, and give meaningful consequences time to emerge. Use paragraph breaks for shifts in action, perception or speaker. With no specified stopping point, finish at a natural pause after the situation develops. Avoid padding with repeated descriptions, circular dialogue or unrelated events.

Use startingState.people[].currentPlace for each character's actual starting place. When known, stage that character's first action there and show their movement before placing them elsewhere. For example, a viewpoint character in the Main House can go out to meet someone in the Yard before either enters the Barn. plan.intendedLocationId is the intended scene focus, which the passage may end before reaching. A shared parent place implies neither co-location nor line of sight. Keep unknown locations unknown. Use only as many characters as the scene needs. With no viewpoint, choose a supplied starting place or open with the setting.

Respect routes, possession and nested inventory. Worn clothes come from items and wear links. Use supplied scene resources where relevant and establish completed changes through concrete action. Apply scene limits to incidental gestures too, including pickups, handoffs and clothing changes. Avoid invented stock counts, offscreen discoveries and adding people, props or destinations for padding. Story records are reference facts. fieldGuidance describes repeated fields by capability and key.
